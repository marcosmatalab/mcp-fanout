# F2: structural containment for number 5. Pre-registration.

Same discipline as F1 (`docs/CALIBRATION.md`): baselines, per-piece predictions and the stop
criterion are written and frozen BEFORE the code exists, so a result cannot quietly become its own
prediction. The block between the markers is hashed by `tests/test_prereg_f2_prediction.py`,
exactly as the phase B predictions are.

Written 2026-09-19, after an external review of the matcher, before any line of `structure.py`.

`make verify` is green at 493 tests at the time of writing.

## 0. The finding that outranks this work, recorded first

The measurement that produced the denominator argument below also produced the strongest result
this project has. It is written up as **`docs/THREATS.md` threat 17** and it is not a matcher
question at all:

`mcp-server-fetch` depends on `readabilipy`, whose `simple_json.py` calls `have_node()` during
HTML conversion, which is to say inside `tools/call`. When `node_modules` is missing that calls
`run_npm_install()`, which runs `subprocess.run(["npm", "install"], check=True)` against a
`package.json` with no lockfile and three unpinned ranges. A tool call downloads and executes
about forty third-party packages, from a registry the tool's documentation never mentions, with
contents that differ between days, with nothing recorded about what ran and nothing shown to the
user.

The 87 connections recorded on 2026-09-18 and the 82 recorded on two separate runs on 2026-09-19
are not a stale figure needing a refresh. Two runs a pass apart agreeing on 82 rules out noise,
and the difference from 87 is the direct measurement of the instability. It is evidence.

This is the leading candidate for the paper's headline, above number 3: number 3 measures a
property of a population, this is a reproducible mechanism with a named causal chain that
generalises to any tool that lazily installs a dependency at call time. Disclosure draft, to both
sets of maintainers, in `docs/disclosure/2026-09-19-readabilipy-npm-install-at-call-time.md`, to
be sent before anything is published (gate rule 7).

## 1. The reserve, and a guard that turned out to be a courtesy

**`corpus/negative/held-out.json` is no longer a reserve.** Scripts supplied with the external
review loaded it with a bare `json.load`, bypassing `calibrate.load_negative`, and measured it.
Its false-positive figure is a CALIBRATION figure from that moment on and is cited as one
everywhere in this document. The material is not wasted: it is still a valid negative control, it
is simply no longer unseen.

**Verdict on the guard: it is a courtesy, not a lock, and it is now written down as one.**
`load_negative` refuses the reserved half to a calibration purpose, and any `json.load` of the
path walks straight past it. Nothing inside a repository can prevent that: the file has to be
readable to be usable, and a determined reader is always one function call away.

What a lock would actually cost, so the choice is priced rather than asserted:

| option | cost | why it was not taken |
|---|---|---|
| keep the reserve out of the repository | the corpus stops being re-derivable and gate rule 1 breaks | reproducibility is the higher rule here |
| encrypt it, key held elsewhere | key management for one file, and the key lives beside it in practice | solves nothing against the actual failure, which was convenience |
| make a bypass DETECTABLE after the fact | one test, and it only catches published figures | **taken** |

So the reserve is guarded the only way it can be: `corpus/negative/reserved.json` states in its
own header that the guard is a courtesy, and
`tests/test_negative_reserve.py::test_no_published_figure_cites_the_reserve_yet` fails if a
document starts quoting a rate from it before the single end-of-work measurement. That is the
difference between a courtesy and a courtesy somebody notices.

**The new reserve.** `corpus/negative/reserved.json`, 40 calls in 5 families, generated by
`tools/build_negative_extras.py` and re-derivable by `--check`. Its information strings appear
nowhere in either older half and its call ids collide with neither. It has never been measured.

**The new family, built against THIS matcher.** `containment_subset`, in both the calibration half
and the reserve. Four cases, as asked: a call whose tokens are a proper subset of another call's
request, a second subset one token longer, two calls sharing an enum ref name and nothing else,
and one repository reached through two organisations.

Building it forced a collision worth recording, because it is a corpus invariant meeting a matcher
it was not written for. `test_every_family_states_what_it_is_for` required every call to declare
information that would make a match a true positive. The subset attack cannot satisfy that: owning
a distinguishing value is exactly what would stop a call being a subset of another's request. The
invariant was not relaxed, it was split: a call may now declare emptiness, in an
`information_free` field, with a reason of more than 120 characters that has to be an argument
rather than a label. Silence is still a failure. Five of the eight calls use it.

## 3. Privacy: checked first, as asked

**Verdict: token digests hold for the measurement and do not hold for a deployment.** The proposal
stands. The consequences are written into `docs/DOCTRINE.md` rather than left in a review thread,
in three new sections: the boundary of negative 3 (structure is decomposed, meaning is not, with
the allowed and forbidden operations listed and the test being whether two independent
implementations must agree); why a digest of a short token is obfuscation and not control; and the
legal status of what is stored.

Three things carried across from the review and strengthened:

1. For short tokens a keyed digest is **obfuscation, not access control**. It raises the cost of a
   casual read and bounds nothing. This is `redact.py` PENDING gap 2 arriving on the main path,
   not a new discovery: that note already names enums and short codes as the class it cannot
   protect, and structural tokens are that class. The 22-byte window had been an accidental
   privacy floor and decomposing removes it.
2. **A per-installation key protects against the outside, and the evidence file lives on the
   inside.** Whoever reads an audit trail in the normal course of their work holds the key by
   construction. PENDING gap 1 is necessary and not sufficient. Both gaps are promoted from
   pre-deployment to-dos to preconditions of deploying this matcher outside a measurement.
3. The **tuple-digest option is recorded and deliberately not chosen**: persist the digest of the
   ordered token tuple plus the count, pair with individual digests only in memory. It defeats the
   dictionary attack on the stored artifact and it breaks containment, which is a subset test
   where a tuple digest supports only equality. Its collision with threat 16 is the real decision
   and it is stated in both directions: a tuple digest makes a match LESS re-derivable by an
   auditor, not more. Neither side has been measured. The choice belongs to whoever owns a
   deployment.

The legal sentence is in the doctrine in these words: a keyed hash of a personal data item is
pseudonymised data, not anonymised data, under recital 26 of the GDPR, and it remains within
scope.

## 4. Rule C, adopted as a principle

Not a token-count threshold. **A call that owns no token distinguishing it from the other calls
active at the same moment leaves the candidate set**, instead of turning the whole wave ambiguous.
Formally: call `c` is a candidate only if `tokens(c)` minus the union of the other active calls'
tokens is non-empty.

Measured over this run's actual waves, from the corpus, before any capture:

| rule | CONTENT_UNIQUE | of which FALSE | AMBIGUOUS | UNATTRIBUTED |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no floor | 20 | **9** | 9 | 7 |
| floor on the smallest token count in the match set | 9 | 0 | 20 | 7 |
| **rule C as a principle** | **17** | **0** | **0** | **19** |

Without it, the site-root call (`{"url": "https://example.net/", "max_length": 2000}`, whose only
token is the host) is contained in every flow of its own wave, and nine `/robots.txt` requests get
credited to it with full confidence. Rule B is safe and throws away two thirds of the gain. Rule C
keeps 17 and invents nothing.

Its cost is real and is stated: at N = 10 the call `/docs/deploy/runbook` is excluded too, because
`/docs/deploy/runbook?section=rollback-steps` is a strict superset of it, so the agent re-reading
its own document costs the first read its attribution. That flow goes UNATTRIBUTED and is never
reassigned, which is the safe direction.

**Two commitments that land with the implementation.**

1. A test that a flow whose only candidate is an excluded call grades UNATTRIBUTED and is never
   reassigned to anyone. The failure mode it guards is the tempting one: falling back to "the only
   call in the window" when discrimination has just rejected that call.
2. `number_5` publishes `non_discriminating_calls`, the count and share of active calls excluded
   for owning no distinguishing token, per wave size. This is a finding about the phenomenon and
   not bookkeeping: it is the rate at which an agent's own concurrent calls become mutually
   indistinguishable, and it is the quantity that decides whether per-call provenance is
   achievable at all at a given concurrency. Measured here: 0 of 2, 0 of 5, **2 of 10**, 0 of 2.

## 5. The npm flows, and what they do to the denominator

`strong_attribution_fraction` divides by `len(run.flows)`. The 127 flows decompose by the run's
own recorded reasons: 82 package infrastructure (threat 17), 7 pre-launch, 38 call-caused and
eligible. The honest fraction is 7 / 38 = 0.1842 against 0.0551 published. Adopted.

What that leaves is not what it looks like:

| class | count | can ever carry argument material? |
|---|---|---|
| `/robots.txt`, 11 bytes, one per fetch call | 17 | **no** |
| `clients2.google.com`, browser update check | 2 | **no** |
| the call's own target | 19 | yes |

Nineteen of the 38 are constant client chatter. The arithmetic ceiling of CONTENT attribution on
the 38-flow denominator is 19 / 38 = 0.5000, and it was 0.5000 before a line of matcher code was
written.

`registry/client-constant-paths.json` is the declared list, shaped like
`package-infrastructure.json`, frozen 2026-09-19, sha256:

    6150f9c722c317c4ec2ab123dac35f8222404ad0172d65755e093778b5da5a79

Any entry added after that date is published as a separate, labelled figure and never folded into
the pre-registered one. One entry is broader than the rule it serves and says so in the file: the
browser update check is excluded by HOST, because the run records `target_bytes` and not the
target, so the constancy of its path was never verified. The two flows and the arithmetic to
remove that exclusion by hand are published with it.

## 6. Predictions, per piece

**P1, `structure.py`.** No figure of its own. Falsified if the same string decomposed as an
argument and as a wire target yields different token sets for any call in `corpus/concurrent/` or
`corpus/negative/`.

**P2, token digests.** Self-match and false-positive figures are IDENTICAL to the plaintext-token
measurement, to the digit. Set equality over digests is set equality over tokens up to a digest
collision, which is the one genuinely probabilistic thing here and is bounded where it already is
(`src/mcpfanout/shingle.py`, the N**2 / 2**62 bound); at these set sizes it is not expected to
fire once. Any divergence is a bug in the digest path, not a property of it. Falsified by any
divergence at all.

**P3, containment as the criterion for number 5.** Self-match rises 0.5000 to 1.0000 on both older
halves and 0.5385 to 1.0000 on the real corpus. False positives on the four ORIGINAL families stay
0.0000. That last figure is explicitly NOT evidence and is not claimed as any: those families were
authored against a k-gram matcher and cannot falsify containment. P5 is where the evidence is.
Falsified if self-match on either older half or on the real corpus lands below 1.0000, or if any
of the four original families produces a false positive under containment.

**P4, rule C as a principle.** On run `20260919T130847Z-concurrent`, re-graded without
re-capturing, strong attribution rises from 7 to 17 flows: 0.4474 on the 38-flow denominator and
**0.8947 on the 19-flow content denominator**. False strong attributions stay at 0, checkable here
because the wave corpus is ground truth. `non_discriminating_calls` reads 0, 0, 2, 0 at wave sizes
2, 5, 10, 2. Falsified if any `/robots.txt` flow grades CONTENT_UNIQUE, if any excluded call is
reassigned a flow, or if strong attribution lands outside 15 to 19 flows.

**P5, the `containment_subset` family.** Measured on the calibration half first and on the reserve
once, at the end. WITHOUT rule C, the family's false-positive rate is strictly greater than
0.0000: it was built so that subset calls are contained in the anchor's request, and a family that
cannot show that is broken rather than passed. WITH rule C, predicted 0.00 to 0.05, because the
principle rejects exactly the calls the subset cases rely on. If the measured rate with rule C
exceeds 0.05, the principle is insufficient on its own, a minimum token count is added on top of
it, and every figure in this document is re-measured at the new floor before anything is
published. A rate of exactly 0.0000 WITHOUT rule C is not a pass: it means the family failed to
attack and it gets rebuilt. Falsified if the family's rate without rule C is 0.0000, or if its
rate with rule C exceeds 0.05 and the added floor fails to bring it back under.

**P6, the split.** Number 4 keeps the exact k-gram matcher and its figures do not move: context
files are prose, their false-positive rate is already 0.0000, and containment has nothing to
decompose there. Falsified if number 4 changes by any amount on the same run.

## 7. Verdict on the INSTRUMENT. Frozen.

Measured on run `20260919T130847Z-concurrent`, re-graded, with the `containment_subset` family in
place and the floor set by P5.

**Denominator:** call-caused, eligible, and not matching
`registry/client-constant-paths.json` at sha256 `6150f9c7...5da5a79`, frozen above before the
measurement. On this run that is 19 flows.

**Threshold: 0.80.** P4 predicts 0.8947.

**Three fractions are published together, always:** over all 127 flows, over the 38 call-caused
eligible flows, and over the content denominator. The first is marked non-comparable. Only the
third binds this verdict. The composition table of section 5 is published with them, so no reader
takes the denominator on trust.

If the instrument fails this threshold, the structural matcher is not worth shipping and the
k-gram matcher stands.

## 8. Verdict on the PRODUCT. Deliberately NOT frozen.

It would be pre-registering a decision about a measurement that has not happened.

Of 127 flows in this run, 108 are machinery: 82 a package manager running inside a tool call, 7
pre-launch, 19 constant client chatter. The phenomenon comes almost entirely from one server.
That is not a property of MCP, it is a property of THIS run, and two threats already say why:

- **Threat 5**, absent credentials change behaviour. Nine of ten servers ran with no credentials
  at all. A server that cannot authenticate cannot reach the destinations it exists to reach, so
  the fan-out measured here is a floor of a floor.
- **Threat 8**, one of ten cannot be measured without credentials and three are abandoned. The
  ten-server registry is not ten comparable servers.

A product verdict drawn from this run would be a verdict about an unauthenticated sample of a
reference server set, stated as a verdict about runtime provenance for agents. So the product
question stays open, and what would close it is named instead: a run where the servers that need
credentials have them, so the flows that carry real arguments to real destinations exist to be
attributed at all. Until then the instrument verdict above is the only one pre-registered, and
the deliverable if the instrument passes is a better measurement, not a decision.

## 9. What this does not answer

Phase C. Containment is measured against a corpus we wrote and waves we sized. The same fragment
across concurrent calls on a real server, pooled connections and delayed egress remain untested,
and nothing here says containment survives them.
